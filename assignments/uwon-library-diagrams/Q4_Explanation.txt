GENERAL RELATIONSHIP (6 sentences)
Entity relationship diagrams describe the structure of persistent data, while dataflow diagrams describe how the system receives, transforms and stores that data. The first connection is between ER entities and DFD data stores: a store can group several related entity types rather than requiring one store per table. The second connection is between ER attributes and the information carried by named data flows, such as identifiers, due times and condition values. The third connection is between ER relationships and the processes that create, validate or change those relationships, such as linking a patron to a copy through a loan. The ER view supplies cardinality and integrity constraints that those processes must preserve. An external Patron or Staff actor in a DFD is a participant role, whereas a PATRON entity in the ER view is the stored record describing a person.

SPECIFIC EXAMPLE: S2 (6 sentences)
In scenario S2, D1 groups PATRON and LOAN_POLICY, D3 groups COPY and DEPARTMENT, D4 stores RESERVATION, and D5 stores LOAN. These are concrete examples of ER entities represented by DFD data stores. The issue and return flows carry loan_id, patron_id, copy_id, due_at and condition values, illustrating the connection between ER attributes and flow contents. Process 2.2 creates the Patron-to-Copy association represented by LOAN, changes the linked reservation to Fulfilled and marks the copy OnLoan. Process 2.3 updates returned_at and condition_in without deleting the loan, preserving the same relationships for borrower traceability. Process 2.4 reads those retained records to provide authorized staff with the copy history, so the event trace, ER view and DFD describe the same lifecycle.

COMPLETENESS (5 sentences)
For these three selected scenarios, I checked every event-trace input, response and persistent state change against a corresponding DFD process, data flow or store. I also checked that every persistent store maps to ER entities, every identifier and stored outcome has an appropriate attribute, and the required relationships have keys and explicit cardinalities. The diagrams include the important refusal paths and integrity rules: facility authorization, duplicate reservations, copy eligibility, loan limits, preserved return history and entitlement checks. Successful and unsuccessful outcomes are distinguished, and no DFD flow connects an external actor directly to a data store. This establishes traceable coverage of the modeled scenarios, not a claim that every future acquisition, registration, supplier or administrative workflow of the entire university has been specified.
